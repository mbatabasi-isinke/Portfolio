✳ CONCEPT STAGE · NOT YET BUILT

EcoGrid Pavement.

Every step powers tomorrow. EcoGrid Pavement is a student concept exploring how a modular walkway could capture a small amount of energy from footsteps and store it for low-power uses, such as an LED security light.

Honest project status

This is a design concept and research project. A working prototype has not yet been built or tested, so energy output, cost, durability, and performance have not been verified.

THE CHALLENGE

Why explore this idea?

Schools and public walkways need reliable lighting, while communities continue to explore practical ways to save energy. EcoGrid asks whether foot traffic could contribute a small amount of locally generated electricity.

  • Explore renewable energy at school scale.
  • Make STEM learning practical and visible.
  • Design a modular walkway concept that can be studied and improved.

THE PROPOSED DESIGN

How it could work

01

Footstep mechanism

A slightly moving surface and mechanical linkage would transfer force from a step.

02

Energy conversion

A suitable generator or piezoelectric element would convert some movement into electricity.

03

Power conditioning

Rectification and a suitable charge-control circuit would manage the output.

04

Storage and use

A correctly selected storage system could supply a low-power LED after safe testing.

POSSIBLE COMPONENTS

What needs research

  • Durable, slip-resistant surface material.
  • Spring or rack-and-pinion mechanism.
  • Generator or piezoelectric transducer.
  • Bridge rectifier and charge controller.
  • Appropriate rechargeable battery and LED load.
  • Protective housing, drainage, and safe wiring.

NEXT STEPS

From sketch to evidence

  • Research component prices and safety requirements.
  • Build a small bench experiment before considering a walkway.
  • Measure voltage, current, and energy under repeatable conditions.
  • Record costs, limitations, and test results honestly.
  • Improve the design based on measured evidence.
